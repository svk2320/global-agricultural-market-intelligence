# Power BI Dashboard Explanation
## Global Agriculture Market Intelligence

---

## 1. Dashboard Overview

### Purpose

The Power BI dashboard is the final consumption layer of the Global Agriculture Market Intelligence Platform. It provides a decision-support view over 20 years (2001–2020) of global agricultural, trade, food security, and commodity price data, allowing users to monitor conditions and identify risk signals at a country level without needing to query the underlying analytical data platform directly.

### Business Problems Addressed

The dashboard is organized around four core business problems:

| # | Business Problem | Business Question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Food Security | Which countries are becoming food insecure? |
| 2 | Agricultural Productivity | Why are some countries producing more than others? |
| 3 | Trade Intelligence | How dependent is each country on imports and exports? |
| 4 | Commodity Price Analysis | Why are agricultural commodity prices changing? |

### Target Users

- Governments & Policymakers
- Agricultural Ministries
- International Organizations (FAO, World Bank, NGOs)
- Agricultural Exporters & Importers
- Commodity Market Analysts
- Agribusiness Companies
- Researchers & Data Analysts

### How the Dashboard Supports Decision Making

Each page is built to answer a specific business question with pre-aggregated metrics, trend lines, and country-level rankings, so that a user can move from "what is happening" (headline KPIs) to "where is it happening" (country breakdowns) to "how has it changed over time" (trend charts) within a single page.

**Important:** Power BI does not directly query raw source data. It consumes curated, pre-aggregated reporting tables stored in PostgreSQL. All business logic, aggregation, and metric calculation happens upstream in the dbt reporting layer — the dashboard's role is strictly visualization and interaction, not computation.

## 2. Dashboard Architecture

### End-to-End Data Flow

```
Raw Source Data
      ↓
DuckDB Data Lake
      ↓
dbt Staging Models
      ↓
dbt Intermediate Models
      ↓
dbt Mart Models
      ↓
dbt Reporting Models
      ↓
PostgreSQL Serving Database
      ↓
Power BI Dashboard
```

### Layer-by-Layer Explanation

**Raw Layer**
Original source datasets are loaded into DuckDB in their native form, without transformation. This layer serves as the immutable data lake for the platform.

**Staging Layer**
Raw datasets are cleaned, standardized, and type-converted here. This includes normalizing column names, correcting data types, and preparing source data for downstream modeling — no business logic is applied at this stage.

**Intermediate Layer**
Reusable transformation logic lives here. Intermediate models combine and reshape staged data into building blocks that multiple downstream marts can share, avoiding duplicated logic across the project.

**Mart Layer**
Business-oriented analytical models are built here, structured around the platform's core subject areas (food security, productivity, trade, prices). Marts represent the "analysis-ready" version of the data, joined and organized around business entities such as country and year.

**Reporting Layer**
Dashboard-ready tables are generated by dbt reporting models. This layer takes the mart-layer data and simplifies and aggregates it specifically for BI consumption — computing the exact KPIs, trend comparisons, and country rankings the dashboard needs. Aggregations and business metrics are prepared *before* reaching Power BI, which improves dashboard performance and keeps the visualization layer free of complex logic.

**PostgreSQL Serving Layer**
Reporting tables produced by dbt are exported into PostgreSQL, which acts as the platform's BI serving database. Power BI connects to PostgreSQL — not to DuckDB or any earlier layer — so the dashboard is fully decoupled from the analytical engine and only ever interacts with lightweight, purpose-built reporting tables.

**Power BI (Visualization Layer)**
Power BI imports data from PostgreSQL's `reporting` schema in Import mode. Reporting tables are optimized datasets with reduced size compared with analytical fact tables, allowing efficient Power BI import and refresh performance.

### Relationship Between Notebooks and Power BI

Analysis notebooks are used for:
- exploratory analysis
- KPI validation
- business question investigation

Power BI does not directly depend on notebooks. Validated analytical logic is converted into dbt reporting models and consumed through PostgreSQL.

---

## Reporting Refresh Flow

1. Source data is loaded into the DuckDB raw layer.
2. dbt transformations update staging, intermediate, and mart models.
3. dbt reporting models generate dashboard-specific aggregates.
4. Reporting tables are exported to PostgreSQL.
5. Power BI refreshes from the PostgreSQL reporting schema.

This separation ensures analytical logic remains version controlled and independent from dashboard development.

## 3. Dashboard Pages

The dashboard consists of five pages: an Overview page plus one page per business problem.

### Overview

A cross-cutting landing page that surfaces one headline KPI per business problem (undernourishment exposure, yield trend, import dependency, price volatility), a normalized multi-metric trend line indexed to 2001 = 100, and a country-coverage summary comparing how many countries are represented across each problem area.

### Food Security Analysis

**Business purpose:** Identify which countries are moving toward food insecurity so that assistance and reserve decisions can be made proactively rather than reactively.

**Questions answered:**
- Which countries need food assistance?
- Where should food reserves be increased?
- Which countries are at risk next year?

**Main KPIs:** Food supply adequacy ratio, count of countries below the minimum adequacy threshold, count of countries with a declining adequacy trend (2001→2020), and population-weighted undernourishment exposure.

**Country-level analysis:** A top/bottom 10 ranking of countries by change in adequacy ratio between 2001 and 2020 highlights both the strongest improvers and the clearest decliners.

**Trends and comparisons:** A global average adequacy ratio trend line (2001–2020) shows the overall trajectory, while a population-weighted vs. simple-average comparison chart distinguishes the experience of the average *person* worldwide from the average *country*.

### Agricultural Productivity Analysis

**Business purpose:** Understand why some countries produce more than others, to guide input investment, irrigation, technology, and crop-selection decisions.

**Productivity measurement:** Centered on wheat yield as the representative crop metric, given its full 2001–2020 country coverage.

**Production and yield analysis:** A global average wheat yield trend line (2001–2020) shows the overall production trajectory, and a top/bottom 10 ranking by percentage change highlights the countries with the largest yield gains and losses.

**Input intensity insights:** Fertilizer intensity (kg/ha) and pesticide intensity (kg/ha) are tracked as headline KPIs and compared side-by-side for the top 10 countries by fertilizer use, giving a view of how input-intensive different agricultural systems are.

**Country comparisons:** The fertilizer-vs-pesticide chart and the yield-change ranking together let users compare countries on both input usage and output outcomes.

### Trade Intelligence

**Business purpose:** Quantify how dependent each country is on imports and exports, to support supplier diversification and new-market decisions.

**Import/export analysis:** Headline KPIs cover average import dependency, export concentration, and import diversification (average number of significant trading partners) for the latest year.

**Trade dependency:** A global average import dependency trend line (2001–2020) shows how reliance on imports has shifted over time, and a top/bottom 10 ranking shows which countries' import dependency has increased or decreased most.

**Trade concentration:** A top 10 ranking of the most export-concentrated countries shows where a single trading partner accounts for a disproportionate share of exports — a key exposure signal.

**Country and commodity comparisons:** Rankings are built at the country level, letting users identify which countries are most exposed to disruption in a single trade relationship.

### Commodity Market Analysis

**Business purpose:** Explain why agricultural commodity prices are changing, to support buy/sell timing and government price-intervention decisions.

**Commodity price trends:** A price volatility trend line (2001–2020) and a three-series index chart (agriculture, food, and fertilizer indices) show how commodity prices have moved over the full period.

**Price movement analysis:** Headline KPIs cover current price volatility and the current agriculture price index level.

**Currency-adjusted insights:** An FX share-of-swing KPI and an accompanying top 10 country ranking isolate how much of a country's local commodity price movement is attributable to currency effects rather than the underlying global commodity price.

**Market risk indicators:** A CPI passthrough (inflation gap) KPI and top 10 ranking show which countries' food inflation diverges most from the global commodity price benchmark — a signal of country-specific economic stress layered on top of global price movements.

## 4. KPI Explanation

| KPI | Business Meaning | Data Source |
|---|---|---|
| Undernourishment Exposure | Population-weighted measure of food supply adequacy, reflecting the average *person* worldwide rather than the average country. A value above 1.0 indicates food energy supply exceeds the minimum requirement for a healthy population. | Reporting table for food security undernourishment |
| Adequacy Ratio | Country-level food supply adequacy ratio; the ratio of average food energy supply to the minimum dietary energy requirement. | Reporting table for food security adequacy ratio |
| Countries Below Threshold | Count of countries whose adequacy ratio falls below the minimum food-security threshold in a given year. | Reporting table for food security threshold status |
| Countries with Declining Adequacy | Count of countries whose adequacy ratio decreased between 2001 and 2020, used as an early-warning risk signal. | Reporting table for food security risk trend |
| Yield Trend (avg % change) | Average percentage change in wheat yield across countries between 2001 and 2020, used as the platform's core productivity indicator. | Reporting table for productivity yield trend |
| Wheat Yield | Country-level wheat yield value for the selected year. | Reporting table for productivity wheat yield |
| Fertilizer Intensity | Average fertilizer application rate per hectare of cropland (kg/ha), used as an input-intensity indicator. | Reporting table for productivity fertilizer intensity |
| Pesticide Intensity | Average pesticide application rate per hectare of cropland (kg/ha), used as an input-intensity indicator. | Reporting table for productivity pesticide intensity |
| Import Dependency | Percentage of a country's key agricultural inputs sourced from imports, indicating exposure to external supply disruption. | Reporting table for trade import dependency |
| Import Dependency Trend | Average percentage-point change in import dependency between 2001 and 2020, used to flag countries becoming more or less reliant on imports over time. | Reporting table for trade dependency trend |
| Export Concentration | Share of a country's exports of a given commodity going to its single largest trading partner; a high value indicates concentrated (risky) trade exposure. | Reporting table for trade export concentration |
| Import Diversification | Average number of "significant" import trading partners a country relies on, used as a supplier-diversification indicator. | Reporting table for trade import diversification |
| Price Volatility | Standard deviation of year-over-year commodity price changes, used as a market risk / timing indicator. | Reporting table for commodity price volatility |
| Agriculture Price Index | Composite index tracking overall agricultural commodity price levels over time, benchmarked against a base year. | Reporting table for commodity price index |
| CPI Passthrough (Inflation Gap) | Average gap between a country's local food inflation and the global commodity price benchmark, indicating country-specific inflationary pressure. | Reporting table for commodity CPI passthrough |
| FX Share of Swing | Share of a country's local commodity price movement attributable to currency (exchange rate) effects rather than the underlying global commodity price. | Reporting table for commodity FX effect |

> KPI names above reflect the descriptive labels used in the dashboard. Exact underlying reporting-table names are not restated here; Section 6 describes the reporting datasets at the dashboard-area level.

## 5. Dashboard Interactions

### Current Dashboard Design

The dashboard is designed as a fixed analytical reporting experience using pre-aggregated reporting tables. Users navigate between dashboard pages to explore different business problem areas:

- Overview
- Food Security Analysis
- Agricultural Productivity Analysis
- Trade Intelligence
- Commodity Market Analysis

Each page presents predefined KPIs, trend analysis, and country-level comparisons based on the reporting models generated through dbt.

### Page Navigation

Users can move between dashboard pages using Power BI page navigation. Each page focuses on a specific analytical question:

- Overview provides a high-level summary across all business areas.
- Food Security focuses on food adequacy and insecurity indicators.
- Agricultural Productivity focuses on yield and input efficiency.
- Trade Intelligence focuses on import dependency and export exposure.
- Commodity Market Analysis focuses on price movement and volatility indicators.

### Visual Interactions

Power BI visual interactions are enabled where applicable. Selecting a data point within a visual can highlight related information across other visuals on the same page.

Examples:

- Selecting a country in a ranking chart highlights related trends.
- Selecting a category in a comparison chart updates related visual emphasis.

### Current Scope Limitation

The current dashboard does not include interactive slicers or user-controlled filters. Analysis is performed through predefined dashboard views and reporting datasets.

Future versions may introduce additional filtering capabilities such as:

- country selection
- region selection
- year range selection
- commodity selection

if more exploratory analysis functionality is required.

## 6. Reporting Tables and Data Sources

### Reporting Data Model

Power BI does not read from marts, intermediate models, or DuckDB directly. It reads exclusively from PostgreSQL reporting tables, which are the output of dbt reporting models. Each reporting table is purpose-built for a specific dashboard KPI or chart, meaning aggregation logic (averages, percentage changes, rankings, threshold checks) is computed once in dbt rather than being recalculated inside Power BI.

| Dashboard Area | Reporting Dataset |
|---|---|
| Food Security | Food security reporting tables |
| Productivity | Productivity reporting tables |
| Trade Intelligence | Trade intelligence reporting tables |
| Commodity Market | Commodity market reporting tables |
| Country Benchmarking | Cross-cutting reporting queries |

All reporting tables live in the PostgreSQL `reporting` schema and are loaded into Power BI in Import mode. Any additional transformation required for dashboard consumption should be implemented in dbt reporting models rather than inside Power BI, keeping analytical logic centralized, version-controlled, and consistent across all visuals.

## 7. Dashboard Validation

The following validation steps were performed to confirm the dashboard reflects the underlying data accurately:

- **KPI values compared against analytical notebooks** — headline figures shown on the dashboard (e.g., undernourishment exposure, yield trend, import dependency, price volatility) were cross-checked against exploratory analysis performed outside Power BI.
- **Reporting tables validated** — reporting-layer output was reviewed for correct grain (one row per country/year as expected) and for consistency with the mart-layer data it was derived from.
- **Aggregations checked** — averages, percentage changes, and threshold counts were spot-checked against manual calculations on a sample of countries and years.
- **Visual interactions tested** — dashboard navigation and visual interactions were reviewed to confirm that page transitions and chart relationships behave as expected.
- **Missing values reviewed** — gaps in country coverage across reporting tables were reviewed so that KPIs and rankings are not silently skewed by uneven data availability.
- **Business logic verified** — metric definitions (e.g., what counts as "below threshold," how percentage change is calculated, how top/bottom rankings are constructed) were reviewed against the original business questions to confirm the dashboard answers them as intended.

## 8. Performance Considerations

- **Pre-aggregated reporting tables** — all computation-heavy logic (averages, percentage changes, rankings) is performed upstream in dbt reporting models, so Power BI never performs heavy aggregation at query time.
- **PostgreSQL serving layer** — acting as a dedicated BI serving database, PostgreSQL isolates the dashboard from the analytical engine (DuckDB) and from the heavier mart-layer models, keeping the tables Power BI queries small and fast.
- **Reduced complexity in Power BI** — because reporting tables are already shaped for specific KPIs and charts, the Power BI data model and DAX logic remain simple, reducing the risk of inconsistent metric definitions across visuals.
- **Separation between analytics and visualization** — the clear boundary between the dbt reporting layer and the Power BI visualization layer means analytical logic can be updated, tested, and version-controlled independently of dashboard design.

## 9. Future Improvements

- **Additional indicators** — expanding coverage beyond wheat to other representative crops, and incorporating additional food security and trade indicators currently out of scope.
- **Forecasting capabilities** — adding forward-looking projections (e.g., near-term food security or price risk) rather than relying solely on historical trend comparisons.
- **More frequent data refresh** — moving from a static 2001–2020 historical load toward a regularly refreshed pipeline as new source data becomes available.
- **Advanced analytics** — incorporating statistical or machine-learning-driven risk scoring in place of simple two-point (2001 vs. 2020) trend comparisons.
- **Additional dashboards** — extending the platform to cover business problems currently out of scope (e.g., climate impact, sustainability, investment prioritization) as data coverage and modeling maturity improve.
